__DENTAVOICE__

FOLLOW THE THREAD

Adaptive Diagnostic Drill Book
One Opener\. Listen\. Follow Her World\.

__Stop sorting people into boxes\. Start following where they lead you\.__

# __THE METHOD: THROW → LISTEN → FOLLOW__

🔑 __*You don't need to figure out what type of person she is before you talk\. You need ONE good opening question\. Her answer tells you everything\. Then you follow HER thread, not your script\.*__

__Step 1: THROW the opener\.__

Same question for every decision\-maker:

__YOU: __"How's your front desk team doing with the workload right now — do they have enough bandwidth, or is it a stretch?"

__Step 2: LISTEN for the thread\.__

Her answer will contain one of these signals:

__She Talks About\.\.\.__

__Her Thread__

__Your Next Questions Are About\.\.\.__

Staff stress, burnout, turnover, being short\-handed

PEOPLE

Team workload, patient complaints, what breaks during rush

Cost, budget, what they pay for services, can't afford to hire

MONEY

Current spending, ROI, cost of inaction, hiring vs\. $297

Herself doing everything, being at the front desk, her own overwhelm

HER DAILY CHAOS

Call volume, voicemails, emotional weight, her workload

The doctor's priorities, what he'd approve, what he cares about

THE DOCTOR

What the doctor worries about, schedule gaps, revenue goals

"That's really a front desk thing" or "I handle billing not phones"

WRONG PERSON

"Who handles the phone and front desk side?" → Get redirected

__Step 3: FOLLOW her thread\.__

Ask your next 2\-3 questions about what SHE brought up\. Stay in her world\. Do not jump to a different topic\. Her thread leads to the same pain — missed calls — through a door SHE opened\.

⚠️ *The moment you leave her thread and jump to YOUR agenda, she feels the shift\. She goes from 'I'm having a conversation' to 'I'm being sold to\.' Stay on HER topic until SHE runs out of things to say\.*

# __THREAD 1: PEOPLE__

__  She said something about staff\. Stay on staff\.__

## __What She Said That Triggered This Thread:__

__THEM: __"We're down a person and my front desk girl is basically doing everything herself\."

OR:

__THEM: __"It's been rough\. We had someone quit last month\."

OR:

__THEM: __"They're stretched thin\. Especially during the morning rush\."

__💡 SIGNAL: __*She mentioned STAFF — headcount, stress, turnover, bandwidth\. This is a People thread\.*

__Follow\-Up 1: Go deeper on staff pressure__

__YOU: __"That's a lot on one person\. When it gets really hectic, what usually gives first — is it the phones that suffer?"

__➡ FOLLOW WITH: __*You're staying on HER topic \(staff overwhelm\) and nudging toward phones\. She opened the door\. You're walking through it gently\.*

__5 REPS__

__Follow\-Up 2: Patient impact__

__YOU: __"Have any patients mentioned trouble getting through on the phone?"

__➡ FOLLOW WITH: __*Still in her world — patient complaints land on HER desk\. She manages that\. Not a data question\. A 'have you heard this' question\.*

__5 REPS__

__Follow\-Up 3: The invisible cost__

__YOU: __"When calls go unanswered during the rush, do you have any sense of how many of those might be new patients?"

__➡ FOLLOW WITH: __*NOW you're bridging from her world \(staff pressure\) to the business impact \(missed new patients\)\. But you arrived here through HER thread, not yours\.*

__5 REPS__

__Follow\-Up 4: The solution frame__

__YOU: __"Has anyone ever talked about bringing on someone just for phones, or looked into any kind of coverage solution?"

__➡ FOLLOW WITH: __*Vendor decisions = her domain\. This naturally leads to: 'We can't afford another hire' which leads to: 'What if you could solve it for under $300/month?'*

__5 REPS__

# __THREAD 2: MONEY__

__THEM: __"We've looked into hiring someone but it's too expensive\."

__THEM: __"We're trying to keep costs down right now\."

__YOU: __"What are you paying for the answering service, if you don't mind me asking?"

OR if no service:

__YOU: __"Yeah, a part\-time hire just for phones is easily $20\-30K a year\. Has anyone looked at tech solutions that cost a fraction of that?"

__Follow\-Up 2: Value\-for\-money question__

__YOU: __"With the answering service — can patients actually book an appointment through them, or do they just take a message?"

__➡ FOLLOW WITH: __*If it's just messages, she's paying $300\+/month for a message pad\. Reframe: same budget, 10x the value\.*

__5 REPS__

__Follow\-Up 3: Cost of inaction__

__YOU: __"Here's what I see with other practices: a missed new patient call costs $800\-1,500 in first\-year revenue\. If even a few slip through per week, the cost of NOT having coverage is way more than the cost of having it\."

__➡ FOLLOW WITH: __*You're not selling expense\. You're selling savings\. She thinks in budget terms, so give her budget math\.*

__5 REPS__

__Follow\-Up 4: The comparison close__

__YOU: __"What if for about the same price as that answering service — or less than one missed patient per month — callers could actually book appointments on the spot? No callback needed\."

__➡ FOLLOW WITH: __*Direct comparison: current spend vs\. your offer\. Same budget, better result\.*

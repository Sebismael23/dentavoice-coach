# __PART 2: YOUR TWO MASTER FRAMEWORKS__

Everything in this document is built on two frameworks\. Understand these deeply and every script will make sense, even when you go off\-script\.

## __Framework 1: Chris Voss — Never Split the Difference__

Chris Voss was the FBI's lead international kidnapping negotiator\. His techniques work in sales because every sales conversation is a negotiation\. Here are the specific techniques woven into every script below:

__Technique 1: Master "No" \(Beware "Yes"\)__

Most salespeople chase "yes\." Voss teaches the opposite\. When someone says "no," they feel safe and in control\. "No" is not rejection — it's the beginning of real conversation\. Your scripts are designed to __invite "no"__ rather than push for "yes\."

__Old way \(bad\):__ "Would you be open to hearing about our service?" → Forces a "yes" that feels like a trap\.

__Voss way \(good\):__ "Would it be a terrible idea if I showed you how this works?" → Invites "no" \("No, it wouldn't be terrible"\) which is actually agreement\.

__Key "no"\-oriented questions you'll use:__

• "Would it be ridiculous to\.\.\." \(Invites "no" = agreement\)

• "Have you given up on solving\.\.\." \(Invites "no" = they still care\)

• "Is it a bad time?" \(Invites "no" = they have time, or honest "yes" = you call back\)

• "Would it be unreasonable to take 10 minutes?" \(Invites "no" = 10 minutes is fine\)

__Technique 2: Mirroring__

Repeat the last 1\-3 critical words someone says, with an upward inflection\. This makes them elaborate, builds rapport, and buys you time to think\.

__THEM: __"We're actually pretty busy with our current system\."

__YOU: __"Your current system?"*  \[Mirror — upward inflection, then silence\]*

__THEM: __"Yeah, we use an answering service but honestly they mess up a lot\.\.\."

They just gave you their pain point without you asking a single probing question\.

__Technique 3: Labeling__

Name the emotion or concern you observe\. Start with "It seems like\.\.\." or "It sounds like\.\.\." Never use "I" — it makes it about you\. Labeling defuses negative emotions and reinforces positive ones\.

__YOU: __"It sounds like your front desk is juggling a lot right now\."*  \[Label\]*

__THEM: __"Oh my God, you have no idea\. Between insurance and patients walking in\.\.\."

Labeling opens the floodgates\. They feel understood\. Now they want to tell you everything\.

__Technique 4: The Accusation Audit__

Before they can object, list every negative thing they might be thinking\. This defuses resistance before it forms\.

__YOU: __"You're probably thinking this is just another sales call, or that AI is going to sound robotic, or that this will be a headache to set up\.\.\."*  \[Accusation audit\]*

__▶ TONE: __*Slow, calm, empathetic\. Late\-night FM DJ voice\.*

When you say their objections out loud first, the objections lose their power\. They often respond with "Actually, it's not that bad" or "Well, tell me more\."

__Technique 5: "That's Right" \(Not "You're Right"\)__

"You're right" means "go away\." "That's right" means breakthrough\. Your goal is to summarize their situation so accurately that they say "That's right\." This creates trust and emotional buy\-in\.

__YOU: __"So it sounds like your front desk handles check\-ins, insurance verification, and phones all at once\. And when they're with a patient, calls just go to voicemail\. And you know some of those are new patients you'll never hear from again\."*  \[Summary for "That's right"\]*

__THEM: __"That's exactly right\."*  \[BREAKTHROUGH — they feel deeply understood\]*

__Technique 6: Calibrated Questions__

Questions that start with "How" or "What" give the other person the illusion of control while you direct the conversation\. Never ask "why" — it feels accusatory\.

• "How do you currently handle calls when everyone's busy?"

• "What happens to those calls after hours?"

• "What would it mean for the practice if you captured every one of those?"

• "How would you like to move forward?"

## __STAGE 1: OPENING / PATTERN INTERRUPT__

__GOAL: __Break their mental pattern of "this is a sales call, hang up\." Sound like a real person\. Earn 10 more seconds\.

__The Opening Line__

__RECEPTIONIST: __"Thank you for calling \[Practice Name\], this is \[Name\], how can I help you?"

__YOU: __"Hey \[Name\] — quick one\."*  \[Pause 1 full second after this\. Do NOT rush into the next line\.\]*

__▶ TONE: __*Casual\. Like you're calling a colleague\. Downward inflection on "quick one\." Slightly slower than normal speech\. Smile while you say it — they can hear it\.*

__🎯 VOSS: __*"Quick one" is a pattern interrupt\. It's unexpected\. It doesn't trigger their "sales call" alarm\. The pause after it creates a micro\-moment of curiosity\.*

__Why This Works__

Most cold callers blast into their pitch: "Hi this is John from XYZ company and we help businesses with\.\.\." The receptionist's brain categorizes this as SPAM in 2 seconds and starts planning their exit\.

"Hey \[Name\] — quick one" sounds like someone who already knows them\. The pause forces them to engage because silence is uncomfortable\.

__If They Say "Hi" or "Yeah?" or Just Wait__

Move to Stage 2 immediately\.

__If They Say "Who is this?" Before You Continue__

__YOU: __"Oh — this is Seb, I'm with DentaVoice\. Not a patient call\. I just had a quick question about how you handle your phones — is this a bad time?"*  \[Voss: "Is this a bad time?" invites "no" = they have time\]*

__▶ TONE: __*Calm, unbothered, like it's no big deal\. Don't sound caught off guard\.*

## __STAGE 2: PERMISSION \+ DIAGNOSIS__

__GOAL: __Ask ONE diagnostic question that reveals pain AND positions you as someone who understands their world\. Not selling yet — just learning\.

__The Diagnostic Question__

__YOU: __"When your front desk is already helping a patient, do calls roll to voicemail, or do they just keep ringing?"

__▶ TONE: __*Genuine curiosity\. Downward inflection\. Slow\. This is NOT a rhetorical question — you actually want to know\.*

__🎯 VOSS: __*This is a calibrated question \(starts with implied "what happens when\.\.\."\)\. It gives them control to answer however they want\. Both answers reveal pain\.*

__💰 HORMOZI: __*You're diagnosing before prescribing\. Doctors don't say "take this pill" before examining\. Asking about THEIR situation first is what separates you from every other cold caller\.*

__Response Path A: They Answer the Question__

__THEM: __"Uh, yeah they go to voicemail usually\."

__YOU: __"Voicemail?"*  \[Mirror — repeat their word with upward inflection, then SILENCE\]*

__▶ TONE: __*Slight surprise, not judgmental\. Then wait\. Let them fill the silence\.*

__THEM: __"Yeah, I mean when we're busy\.\.\. we try to call back but sometimes we don't get to it\."

__YOU: __"It sounds like you're juggling a lot up there\."*  \[Label — name what you observe\]*

__THEM: __"Oh you have NO idea\. Between check\-ins, insurance, and the phone\.\.\."

__They just told you their pain without you asking "what's your biggest challenge?"__ Mirroring \+ labeling did the work\.

__Response Path B: They Get Suspicious__

__THEM: __"Why are you asking?"

__YOU: __"Fair question\. I help dental practices capture calls that slip through when the front desk is tied up\. I'm not trying to sell you anything right now — I'm just trying to understand if it's even an issue for you guys\."*  \[Honest, no games\]*

__▶ TONE: __*Direct, calm, zero pressure\. The phrase "not trying to sell you anything right now" is critical\. "Right now" is honest — you WILL sell eventually\. But not in this moment\.*

__Response Path C: They Say "We're Fine"__

__THEM: __"Oh we're fine\. Everything goes through our system\."

__YOU: __"That's good to hear\. So nothing rolls to voicemail when you're all with patients?"*  \[Voss: gentle challenge framed as confirmation\]*

Two things happen here: Either \(A\) they confirm they really do have it handled — thank them and move on\. Or \(B\) they pause and reveal cracks: "Well\.\.\. I mean, sometimes during lunch\.\.\."

## __STAGE 3: NAVIGATE THE GATEKEEPER__

__GOAL: __Get transferred to the decision maker \(dentist or office manager\)\. OR recruit the receptionist as your internal champion\. Never bypass — always recruit\.

__Critical Mindset Shift__

__The receptionist is NOT your enemy\.__ She IS your product's primary beneficiary\. She's the one drowning in calls while checking in patients\. If you position correctly, she will WANT this to exist and will champion it internally\.

Your approach with the receptionist integrates two Hormozi principles: \(1\) reduce their effort/sacrifice — you're making their job easier, not replacing them, and \(2\) dream outcome — imagine a day where you don't have to rush patients because the phone is ringing\.

__Asking for the Transfer__

__YOU: __"That makes total sense\. Hey, who usually handles decisions about phone systems or office tools? Is that Dr\. \[Name\] or is there an office manager?"

__▶ TONE: __*Casual\. "Hey" keeps it conversational\. You're asking for DIRECTION, not permission\.*

__Path A: "They're with a patient"__

__THEM: __"Dr\. Smith is with a patient right now\."

__YOU: __"With a patient right now?"*  \[Mirror\]*

__THEM: __"Yeah, they're booked solid until 3\."

__YOU: __"Got it\. Would it be unreasonable for me to try back around 3:15?"*  \[Voss: "unreasonable" invites "no" = it's fine\]*

__THEM: __"No, that should be okay\."

__YOU: __"Perfect\. And just so I don't catch you at a bad time again — is there a better number or should I call this line?"

You got a callback time, the right number, AND the receptionist now expects your call\. You're no longer a cold caller at 3:15 — you're the person she told Dr\. Smith would be calling\.

__Path B: Hard Gatekeeper \("We don't take sales calls"\)__

__THEM: __"We don't take sales calls\."

__YOU: __"Totally fair\."*  \[Pause 2 seconds\. Do not immediately counter\.\]*

__▶ TONE: __*Warm\. No pushback energy\. Voss late\-night FM DJ voice\.*

__YOU: __"It sounds like you get a lot of these\."*  \[Label\]*

__THEM: __"Yeah, we get calls all day from people trying to sell us stuff\."

__YOU: __"All day?"*  \[Mirror\]*

__THEM: __"Seriously\. It's like five a day sometimes\."

__YOU: __"That must be exhausting on top of everything else you're handling\. Look, I'm not going to waste your time\. Can I just send a one\-page info sheet to the office email? If it's not relevant, delete it\. No follow\-up calls\."*  \[Label \+ low\-commitment ask\]*

__THEM: __"Sure, it's info@\.\.\."

__🎯 VOSS: __*You didn't fight the gatekeeper\. You validated her frustration \(label\), showed you're different \(no follow\-up pressure\), and got an email to follow up with a warm lead\. This email is now a warm touch, not a cold email\.*

__Path C: Receptionist Is Interested Herself__

__THEM: __"Wait, what is it exactly? Because we actually do miss calls sometimes\.\.\."

__YOU: __"Oh really? What happens when that occurs?"*  \[Calibrated question — let THEM describe the pain\]*

If the receptionist starts sharing pain points, let her talk\. She may become your biggest advocate\. After she vents:

__YOU: __"It sounds like this is something that actually affects your day\-to\-day\. Would it be a terrible idea if I put together a quick summary of how we handle this for other practices? You could show it to Dr\. \[Name\] when there's a quiet moment\."*  \[Voss: "terrible idea" invites "no" = it's a good idea\]*

__💰 HORMOZI: __*You just turned the receptionist into your internal salesperson\. She will describe the problem AND your solution to the dentist in her own words, which is 10x more powerful than you saying it\.*

## __STAGE 4: RESET WITH DECISION MAKER__

__GOAL: __Re\-introduce yourself, establish credibility in 10 seconds, and earn 90 more seconds of their attention\. Dentists will give you exactly one chance — make it count\.

__The Reset Opening__

__YOU: __"Dr\. \[Name\], thanks for taking a second\. I'll be quick — I know you've got patients\."*  \[Respect their time immediately\]*

__▶ TONE: __*Confident, not timid\. You're a professional with something valuable, not a beggar hoping for time\. Slow pace\. Downward inflection\.*

__YOU: __"I'm Seb with DentaVoice\. I work with small dental practices here in Salt Lake on a specific problem: the calls that get missed when the front desk is busy with patients\."*  \[One sentence\. Clear\. Local\. Specific\.\]*

__YOU: __"\[Name\] at your front desk actually mentioned this might be relevant\. Would it be a terrible idea if I took 90 seconds to explain what we do?"*  \[Social proof from receptionist \+ Voss "terrible idea" close\]*

__🎯 VOSS: __*"Would it be a terrible idea" is one of the most powerful Voss phrases\. It invites "no" \("No, go ahead"\) which is psychologically easier for them than saying "yes\." Plus you're anchoring at 90 seconds — absurdly short, which makes it feel low\-risk\.*

__If the Receptionist Didn't Warm Them Up__

__YOU: __"Dr\. \[Name\], I'll be super brief\. I'm Seb with DentaVoice — I help dental practices in Salt Lake capture calls that get missed when the front desk is slammed\. Is this a bad time, or can I take 60 seconds?"*  \[Voss: "is this a bad time" invites "no" = it's fine\]*

If they say "It IS a bad time": "Totally get it\. When's a better time to call back — early morning or end of day?" Lock in a specific time\. Don't accept "call me sometime\."

## __STAGE 5: PAIN DISCOVERY__

__GOAL: __Uncover their specific missed\-call pain so deeply that THEY convince THEMSELVES they need a solution\. You ask\. They sell\. The more they talk about the problem, the more urgently they want it fixed\.

__The Discovery Sequence \(3 Questions\)__

You don't need 10 questions\. You need 3 great ones, with mirrors and labels between them\.

__Question 1: Current Situation__

__YOU: __"Walk me through what happens when a call comes in and your front desk is already with a patient\."*  \[Calibrated "what" question\]*

__▶ TONE: __*Genuine curiosity\. Lean back energy\. You're a consultant diagnosing, not a salesperson probing\.*

*Let them talk\. Mirror key phrases\. Label emotions\.*

__Question 2: Impact__

__YOU: __"When those calls go to voicemail\.\.\. what percentage would you say actually call back?"*  \[Quantify the pain\]*

They'll usually say something like "maybe half" or "not many\." Then:

__YOU: __"Not many\.\.\."*  \[Mirror — let them sit with it\]*

__THEM: __"Yeah, honestly we probably lose some new patients that way\."

__YOU: __"It sounds like that's been a frustration for a while\."*  \[Label\]*

__Question 3: Dream Outcome \(Hormozi\)__

__YOU: __"What would it mean for the practice if every single one of those calls was answered and handled — even when your team is completely tied up?"

__▶ TONE: __*Slower\. Let the dream outcome land\. This is the moment they start imagining a better reality\.*

__💰 HORMOZI: __*This is the Dream Outcome lever\. You're making them visualize the end state before you've said a word about your product\. When they describe what it would mean — more patients, less stress, better reviews — they're selling themselves\.*

__Going for "That's Right"__

After they've shared their situation, summarize everything they told you:

__YOU: __"So let me make sure I've got this right\. Your front desk handles check\-ins, insurance, AND phones\. When they're with a patient, calls go to voicemail\. Not all of those people call back\. And you know some of those are new patients you'll never hear from again\. That has to be frustrating\."*  \[Summary \+ label for "That's right"\]*

__THEM: __"That's exactly right\."

__🎯 VOSS: __*When they say "that's right" — not "you're right" — you have emotional buy\-in\. They feel deeply understood\. This is the most important moment of the entire call\. Everything after this is downhill\.*

## __STAGE 6: ACCUSATION AUDIT \+ OFFER__

__GOAL: __Defuse every possible objection BEFORE presenting the offer\. Then present the Grand Slam Offer in a way that makes the price irrelevant compared to the value\.

__The Accusation Audit__

__YOU: __"Before I go any further — you're probably thinking a few things\."*  \[Pause\]*

__YOU: __"Or it's going to be a nightmare to set up\.\.\."*  \[Name fear \#2\]*

__YOU: __"Or there's some crazy contract you'll get locked into\.\.\."*  \[Name fear \#3\]*

__YOU: __"Am I off base?"*  \[Invite them to correct you\]*

__▶ TONE: __*Empathetic, slow, calm\. You're showing you understand their world\. Not rushing\. Late\-night FM DJ voice\.*

__🎯 VOSS: __*The accusation audit lists every negative thought they might have BEFORE they voice it\. When you say it first, it loses its power\. Most people respond with "Well, I was wondering about the AI part\.\.\." which gives you a specific concern to address rather than vague resistance\.*

Common responses after accusation audit:

__THEM: __"I mean, yeah, I was wondering about the AI part\."

__YOU: __"That's the number one thing everyone asks\. Here's the honest answer — patients can't tell the difference\. __The Grand Slam Offer Presentation__

Now you present the offer using Hormozi's value stack\. You're not selling "AI phone system\." You're selling a specific, risk\-free outcome\.

__YOU: __"I handle the entire setup\. Your team doesn't lift a finger\. We go live within 48 hours\."*  \[Minimize time delay \+ effort\]*

__YOU: __"And here's the thing — I'm offering a free 14\-day pilot\. No contract, no credit card\. If it doesn't capture at least 10 patient inquiries in those two weeks, we shut it off and you owe me nothing\."*  \[Risk reversal \+ guarantee\]*

__▶ TONE: __*Slower on the guarantee\. Let it land\. This is the moment the deal tips\.*

__💰 HORMOZI: __*You just hit all four levers: Dream Outcome \(capture every call\), Perceived Likelihood \(free pilot, guarantee\), Time Delay \(48 hours\), Effort \(you handle everything\)\. The perceived value is $3,000\+/month\. The ask is zero\.*

__YOU: __"Would it be ridiculous to try it for two weeks and see the data?"*  \[Voss: "ridiculous" invites "no" = it's reasonable\]*

## __STAGE 7: OBJECTION HANDLING__

__GOAL: __Navigate resistance using Voss techniques\. Every objection is information, not rejection\. Your job: understand what's behind the objection and address the REAL concern\.

__The Universal Objection Framework \(works for ANY objection\):__

1\. Pause\. Don't react immediately\.

2\. Label: "It sounds like\.\.\."

3\. Mirror or ask a calibrated question to go deeper\.

4\. Address the real concern\.

5\. Re\-present the offer or adjust your ask\.

__Objection: "We're not interested"__

__THEM: __"We're not interested\."

__YOU: __"Totally fair\."*  \[Pause 2 seconds\. Calm\. No pushback\.\]*

__YOU: __"Have you given up on trying to capture those missed calls, or is it just not the right time?"*  \[Voss: "given up" invites "no" — they haven't given up\]*

__Path A: "We just don't need it"__

__THEM: __"We just don't really have that problem\."

__YOU: __"That's great\. Not every practice does\. Out of curiosity — what happens to calls during lunch hour?"

If they admit calls slip through → continue with discovery\. If they genuinely don't have the problem → thank them, leave clean\.

__Path B: "Not the right time"__

__THEM: __"It's just not the right time right now\."

__YOU: __"Not the right time\.\.\."*  \[Mirror\]*

__THEM: __"Yeah, we're dealing with \[staffing/renovation/whatever\]\."

__YOU: __"It sounds like you've got a lot on your plate\. Would it be unreasonable if I checked back in 3\-4 weeks?"

Get a specific date\. Mark your calendar\. This is now a warm callback, not a cold call\.

__Objection: "How much does it cost?"__

__THEM: __"What does this cost?"

__YOU: __"Great question\. After the pilot, if you decide to keep it, it's $197\-497 a month depending on call volume\. But here's the thing — a single new patient covers 2\-3 months of the service\. And the pilot is completely free\."*  \[Hormozi: anchor value against cost\]*

__If they flinch at the price:__

__THEM: __"That's a lot\."

__YOU: __"It sounds like the price is a concern\."*  \[Label — don't argue\]*

__THEM: __"I mean, yeah, we're watching expenses\."

__YOU: __"Totally get that\. Let me ask you this — how much is a new patient worth to your practice? Like average lifetime value?"

__THEM: __"I don't know\.\.\. probably a few thousand\."

__YOU: __"So if this captures even one patient a month that you would have missed\.\.\. it pays for itself several times over\. That's why we do the pilot first — so you can see the actual numbers before spending a dime\."*  \[Hormozi: ROI framing\]*

__Objection: "Send me some info"__

This is the \#1 brush\-off in cold calling\. It almost never means they want info\. It means "I want to get off the phone\." Here's how to handle it:

__THEM: __"Can you just send me some info?"

__YOU: __"Happy to\."*  \[Don't fight it\. Agree first\.\]*

__YOU: __"Just so I send something relevant and not waste your time — are missed calls currently costing you appointments, or is it more about after\-hours coverage?"*  \[Calibrated question to re\-engage\]*

Two things happen: \(A\) They answer the question, which means they're now in a real conversation — the brush\-off failed and you're back on track\. Or \(B\) They insist: "Just send whatever you have\."

__If they insist:__

__YOU: __"Absolutely\. What's the best email? And would it be unreasonable if I followed up Thursday to see if it's worth a 10\-minute look?"*  \[Get email \+ callback commitment\]*

Now you have an email for warm follow\-up AND a specific day to call back when you're no longer cold — you're "the guy who sent the info\."

__Objection: "I need to talk to my partner/the other doctor"__

__THEM: __"I'd need to talk to my partner about this\."

__YOU: __"Of course\."*  \[Pause\]*

__YOU: __"What do you think they'd be most concerned about?"*  \[Calibrated question — uncovers the REAL objection through their partner\]*

__THEM: __"He's pretty skeptical about new technology\."

__YOU: __"Skeptical about new technology\.\.\."*  \[Mirror\]*

__THEM: __"Yeah, we tried some software last year that was a mess\."

__YOU: __"It sounds like that experience left a bad taste\. That's actually why we do the free pilot — no commitment, no contract\. If it doesn't work, we stop\. Would it make sense for me to hop on a quick call with both of you? That way I can address his concerns directly\."

__🎯 VOSS: __*Asking "what would they be concerned about" gets them to voice objections they themselves might have but haven't said\. The mirror pulls out the full story\. The label validates the concern\. Then you offer the solution\.*

__Objection: "Is this AI? / Will it sound robotic?"__

__THEM: __"Wait, is this like an AI? Won't it sound like a robot?"

__YOU: __"It is AI — and that's the number one thing everyone asks\."*  \[Validate the concern\]*

__YOU: __"Here's the honest truth: patients can't tell\. It uses the same technology as \[natural conversation AI\]\. It knows your hours, your insurance, your appointment types\. But you shouldn't take my word for it — that's exactly why we pilot first\. You hear it on real calls with real patients and decide for yourself\."*  \[Hormozi: reduce perceived risk\]*

__GOAL: __Secure a specific commitment \(pilot start date, demo call\) with concrete next steps\. Never leave a call with "I'll be in touch\."

__YOU: __"Perfect\. Here's what happens next\. I'll send you a calendar link for a 15\-minute setup call where I learn your hours, appointment types, and protocols\. After that, I handle everything\. You'll be live within 48 hours\."*  \[Clear next steps — Hormozi: minimize effort\]*

__YOU: __"Great\. I'll send that in the next 30 minutes\. If you need to reschedule, just let me know — I'm flexible\."*  \[Time\-bound commitment\]*

__YOU: __"Absolutely\. Let's do a 15\-minute demo\. I'll call your office as the AI so you can hear exactly what your patients would experience\. What day works this week — Wednesday or Thursday?"*  \[Two\-option close\]*

# __PART 9: MINDSET & PERFORMANCE COACHING__

## __Reframe: You Are Not Selling__

Hormozi says it best: if your product genuinely helps people, you have a moral obligation to sell it\. Every dental practice that misses calls is losing patients\. Some of those patients are in pain and need help\. You have the solution sitting on your laptop\. Not offering it is the selfish act\. Offering it is service\.

## __The 60\-Second Focus Routine \(Before Every Call Session\)__

1\. Stand tall\. Feet shoulder\-width\. Hands at sides or on hips\.

2\. Breathe in slowly for 4 counts through your nose\.

3\. Hold for 4 counts\.

4\. Breathe out slowly for 6 counts through your mouth\.

5\. Repeat 3 times \(total: ~45 seconds\)\.

6\. Say your micro\-prompt out loud \(see below\)\.

7\. Smile\. Dial\.

## __Micro\-Prompts for Common Doubts__

## __Decision Rules__

__Rule 1: Never spend more than 30 minutes preparing before executing\.__ If you're spending more than 30 minutes "getting ready" before calling, you're procrastinating\. Set a timer\. When it goes off, dial\.

__Rule 2: 30 calls \+ 15 emails per week is non\-negotiable\.__ This is the minimum effective dose\. Below this, you won't generate enough data to learn or enough opportunities to convert\.

__Rule 3: If a call goes badly, dial the next number within 60 seconds\.__ Don't analyze mid\-session\. Don't spiral\. Dial\. Analyze at the end of the day\.

__Rule 4: Sunday evening = 30\-minute weekly review\.__ What worked? What didn't? One thing to improve\. That's it\.

__Rule 5: Track everything\.__ If you can't measure it, you can't improve it\. Every call, email, response, demo, and outcome goes in the tracker\.
